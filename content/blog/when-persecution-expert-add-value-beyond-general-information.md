General country information can provide important background in an asylum or immigration case. However, it may not always address every question arising from an individual's particular circumstances.

Country reports, background material and other published sources can help explain conditions in a particular country. A persecution expert may, where appropriate, provide more focused analysis of how those conditions relate to a particular profile, allegation or disputed evidential issue.

A persecution expert does not determine whether an applicant is telling the truth or decide the outcome of an asylum claim. The purpose of specialist evidence is generally to address matters within the expert's area of expertise and assist the relevant decision-maker with issues requiring specialist knowledge.

## What General Country Information Can Show

General country information can help establish the broader context in which an asylum claim arises.

For example, available country material may provide information about:

- Treatment of particular political, religious, ethnic or social groups  
- Laws and policies affecting particular groups  
- Reported discrimination, violence or other forms of adverse treatment  
- The activities of state authorities  
- The activities of non-state groups  
- Access to relevant institutions or forms of protection  
- Conditions in particular regions or communities

This information can be useful when considering the background to an individual's account.

However, general country information is normally intended to describe conditions at a broader level. It may not address every combination of personal characteristics, circumstances or alleged experiences that can arise in an individual case.

## What a Persecution Expert May Add

Where a case raises a question requiring specialist knowledge, a persecution expert may be able to provide more focused analysis.

Depending on the expert's qualifications, expertise and instructions, this could include consideration of:

- The treatment of a particular profile within a specific country or region  
- The potential significance of particular characteristics or activities  
- How different aspects of an individual's profile may interact  
- Whether available country material addresses a particular factual scenario  
- Relevant country context surrounding an alleged form of persecution  
- Issues concerning political, religious, gender-based, LGBTQ+ or other forms of alleged persecution

The precise scope of any opinion will depend on the subject matter, the expert's expertise, the instructions received and the evidence available for consideration.

## General Country Evidence and Case-Specific Analysis

General country evidence and specialist expert analysis can serve different purposes.

### General country information

General country information may help address questions such as:

- What laws or policies apply in a particular country?  
- How are particular groups generally treated?  
- What forms of discrimination or violence have been reported?  
- What institutions or forms of protection are available?  
- Are there relevant differences between particular regions?

### Case-specific expert analysis

A persecution expert may instead be asked to consider questions such as:

- How might the treatment of a particular profile be understood in the relevant country context?  
- Are particular characteristics relevant to the issue being examined?  
- Does the available country material address the specific circumstances raised in the case?  
- Are there relevant differences between treatment in different locations or communities?  
- What country-specific context may be relevant to a disputed evidential issue?

These questions should remain within the expert's area of specialist knowledge.

An expert would not normally be responsible for deciding disputed facts or determining the legal outcome of an asylum claim. Those matters remain for the appropriate decision-maker.

## When General Country Information May Not Address the Full Question

There may be cases where published country information provides useful background but does not address a particular issue in sufficient detail.

For example, an asylum claim may concern an individual with a combination of characteristics that is not specifically discussed in available reports. The person may also have lived in a particular locality, belonged to a particular organisation, followed a particular religious practice or had a particular public profile.

In circumstances such as these, a specialist may potentially be asked to consider whether the available country evidence provides sufficient context for the specific question under consideration.

This does not necessarily mean that general country information is inadequate. Rather, it may indicate that additional specialist analysis could be relevant to a particular evidential issue.

## The Importance of the Individual Profile

Persecution analysis may involve more than identifying whether a particular group experiences difficulties in a country.

An individual's profile can potentially include several relevant features, such as:

- Political activity  
- Religious identity or practice  
- Gender  
- Sexual orientation or gender identity  
- Ethnic or social background  
- Family or community connections  
- Employment or professional activities  
- Previous association with particular organisations  
- Public or online activities

The relevance of these factors will vary from case to case.

A persecution expert may therefore be instructed to consider the country context surrounding a particular profile rather than simply provide a general description of conditions.

## When Multiple Factors Are Relevant

Some cases involve more than one characteristic or alleged reason for adverse treatment.

For example, an individual may describe concerns connected with both political activity and religious identity. Another case may involve gender or family circumstances alongside allegations involving a non-state group.

General country sources may discuss individual issues separately. Where appropriate and within their expertise, a persecution expert may be asked to consider whether the interaction between relevant factors has particular significance in the country context.

Any such analysis should distinguish between established information, disputed factual matters and assumptions provided for the purpose of the opinion.

## A Persecution Expert Does Not Replace General Country Evidence

A specialist persecution report should not necessarily be viewed as an alternative to general country information.

Depending on the circumstances, the two forms of evidence may complement one another:

1. General country information can provide the wider background.  
2. Evidence about the individual's circumstances can identify the particular issues in dispute.  
3. A specialist expert may provide focused analysis where a question falls within their expertise.  
4. The relevant decision-maker considers the evidence as part of the overall case.

The relevance and weight given to particular evidence will depend on the circumstances of the case and the decision-maker's assessment.

## When a Solicitor May Consider Instructing a Persecution Expert

Before seeking a persecution expert report, a legal representative may wish to identify the precise evidential question requiring specialist input.

### Identify the unresolved issue

Consider what question remains after reviewing the available country information and other evidence.

A focused instruction may be more useful than a broad request for a general report on persecution in a particular country.

### Consider the expert's area of expertise

The expert should have relevant knowledge and experience for the specific subject on which an opinion is sought.

The appropriate expertise may differ depending on whether the case concerns political activity, religion, gender, sexual orientation, a particular social group, non-state actors or another issue.

### Provide relevant materials

The analysis will generally depend on the information and materials made available to the expert.

Instructions should distinguish, where possible, between established information, disputed matters and factual assumptions on which the expert is being asked to comment.

### Keep legal and expert questions distinct

An expert can provide an opinion on matters within their specialist knowledge. Legal representatives should distinguish these matters from legal questions and ultimate determinations that fall to the tribunal or other relevant decision-maker.

This can help keep a persecution expert report focused on the specialist evidence required for the case.

## Questions a Persecution Expert May Be Asked to Address

The appropriate questions will depend on the individual case and the expert's field of expertise.

They may include:

- What is the relevant country context for the individual's particular profile?  
- How are people with the relevant characteristics reported to be treated in the relevant location?  
- Are there documented differences between treatment in different regions?  
- How might particular political, religious or social characteristics be understood in the relevant country context?  
- Does available country information address the specific circumstances raised?  
- Are there contextual factors that may not be apparent from general country material?

These questions should be framed carefully and should not ask the expert to determine matters outside their expertise.

## How Specialist Evidence May Fit Into an Asylum Case

The relevance of a persecution expert report will depend on the issues raised by the individual case.

Where general country evidence already addresses a particular question clearly, additional specialist evidence may not necessarily be required. In other circumstances, a specific evidential issue may call for analysis from someone with relevant specialist knowledge.

This makes the scope of the instruction important. A well-defined instruction can help identify the precise issue on which specialist analysis is sought and the materials that should be considered.

## Conclusion

General country information can provide an important foundation for understanding conditions relevant to an asylum claim. However, it may not always address a particular question arising from an individual's profile, circumstances or alleged risk in sufficient detail.

Where appropriate, a persecution expert may provide more focused analysis of a country-specific or profile-specific issue that falls within the expert's specialist knowledge.

The usefulness of such evidence depends on factors including the expert's expertise, the questions asked, the information available and the issues that need to be addressed. A specialist report is not a substitute for general country evidence, legal analysis or the decision-making function of the relevant tribunal.
