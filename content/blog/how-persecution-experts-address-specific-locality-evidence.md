Country information can play an important role in asylum claims, particularly where an applicant's circumstances are connected to a specific region, city, town or locality. Although national reports may provide useful background, conditions and experiences can differ between locations within the same country.

Where a case raises a specific question about local conditions, a persecution expert may be able to provide specialist analysis of the available country evidence. The relevance of that analysis depends on the issues in dispute, the expert's area of expertise and the quality and scope of the available information.

This article explains how region-specific country evidence may be relevant to an asylum claim, when specialist analysis may be appropriate, and what legal representatives may wish to consider before instructing a persecution expert.

## Why Regional Differences Can Matter in an Asylum Claim

Country conditions are not necessarily uniform across an entire country. Differences in local administration, security arrangements, social attitudes, the presence of particular groups and the conduct of local authorities may affect how individuals experience their circumstances.

For example, information about the treatment of a religious minority at national level may not fully explain the circumstances of that minority in a particular province or community. Similarly, reports about political opposition may describe differences in how authorities respond to political activity across different regions.

Depending on the case, relevant regional factors may include:

- The presence and influence of particular political, religious or social groups
- The activities of local authorities or security forces
- The conduct of non-state actors in a particular area
- Local patterns of discrimination, intimidation or reported violence
- Differences in the implementation of national laws or policies
- Access to relevant institutions and protection mechanisms
- Social or community conditions that may affect a particular profile

These factors should not be assumed to apply in every location or to every individual. Their relevance requires consideration of the available evidence and the specific question raised by the case.

## The Limits of National-Level Country Information

National country reports can provide an important overview of political, legal, social and security conditions. They may also contain regional material, but the level of detail can vary according to the subject, sources and period covered.

A national report may describe a general pattern of treatment without examining the circumstances of a particular locality in depth. In other cases, the available material may address the relevant region but leave questions about a particular group, activity or alleged form of persecution unresolved.

This does not automatically mean that the country information is inadequate. It means that legal representatives may need to consider whether the material addresses the specific evidential question arising in the appeal.

For example, a report describing restrictions on political activity nationally may not establish how a particular local authority treats individuals associated with a particular opposition movement. Equally, evidence of adverse treatment in one city should not automatically be treated as proof that the same conditions exist throughout the country.

The scope and limitations of the evidence are therefore important when assessing its relevance to an individual case.

## What a Persecution Expert May Contribute to Regional Analysis

Where a case raises a question requiring specialist knowledge, a persecution expert may be instructed to examine relevant country evidence concerning a particular region or locality.

The nature of the analysis will depend on the expert's qualifications, expertise and instructions. Potential areas of consideration may include the following.

### Local patterns of treatment

An expert may be asked to examine available information about how people with particular characteristics or profiles are treated in a specified area.

This could involve reported treatment of political activists, religious minorities, members of a particular social group or individuals associated with a specific organisation.

The analysis should identify what the available sources support and avoid treating isolated incidents as conclusive evidence of a general pattern.

### Differences between regions

A country may contain significant regional differences in governance, security, social conditions or the activities of particular groups.

An expert may be able to explain documented differences between locations and consider whether those differences are relevant to the particular issue raised in the case.

Such analysis should distinguish between evidence of a general regional pattern and information relating only to a specific incident, group or locality.

### The activities of local actors

In some cases, the alleged source of harm may operate primarily within a particular area.

Relevant country evidence may concern local officials, security personnel, community organisations, armed groups or other non-state actors. A persecution expert may be asked to examine information about the activities of such actors where that subject falls within their expertise.

The expert's role is to provide specialist context rather than determine whether the applicant's account of particular events is true.

### The relevance of local context to a particular profile

A person's circumstances may be connected to a specific location through residence, employment, political activity, religious practice, family relationships or community involvement.

Where relevant evidence exists, an expert may be asked to explain how the local context relates to the profile or circumstances described in the instructions.

The analysis should remain proportionate to the evidence available and identify any material gaps or uncertainties.

## When Region-Specific Expert Evidence May Be Relevant

The need for specialist analysis depends on the issues raised by the individual claim. A report focused on a particular locality may be worth considering where the available evidence does not adequately address a material question requiring specialist knowledge.

Potential examples include the following.

### Alleged persecution by local authorities

An applicant may describe adverse treatment by officials or security personnel operating in a particular area. National-level material may explain the broader legal or political context, while additional evidence may be needed to understand the reported conduct of the relevant local authorities.

A persecution expert may be asked to examine available information about local practices, relevant institutions or documented patterns of treatment, provided these matters fall within the expert's expertise.

### Alleged harm by a non-state actor

A claim may concern a group whose activities or influence are concentrated in a particular region.

Relevant questions could include what reliable sources report about the group's presence, activities or treatment of people with particular characteristics in that area.

The presence of a group in a locality does not, by itself, establish that every person with a particular profile faces persecution. Any analysis should take account of the specific issue, the available evidence and its limitations.

### Religious or social persecution in a particular community

An applicant may describe difficulties connected with religious practice, social identity or community relationships in a particular locality.

The wider national position may not fully explain local circumstances. Depending on the available sources, specialist analysis may help clarify documented differences in treatment between communities or regions.

The report should not assume that local attitudes are uniform or that an experience described in one community necessarily reflects conditions elsewhere.

### Regional differences in the treatment of political activity

The treatment of political activity may vary according to location, the nature of the activity and the relevant authorities or groups involved.

Where the case raises a specific question about a region, a persecution expert may be asked to examine reliable information about local political conditions and the treatment of people with a relevant profile.

Any conclusions should reflect the scope and reliability of the available evidence rather than generalise from a limited number of reports.

## Distinguishing Evidence About a Region From Evidence About an Individual

One important distinction is between information showing that a particular problem has been reported in a region and evidence concerning an individual's own circumstances.

For example, sources may document violence against a particular group in one province. That information may provide relevant context, but it does not necessarily establish that every member of the group experiences the same treatment or faces the same level of risk.

Likewise, evidence that a local authority has acted against particular individuals does not automatically establish how it would respond to another person with different circumstances.

A persecution expert may help explain the significance and limitations of the available country material. However, the expert should not be asked to convert general regional information into a definitive finding about an applicant's personal experiences or the outcome of their claim.

The decision-maker remains responsible for assessing the evidence as a whole and determining the relevant facts and legal issues.

## Source Quality and the Limits of Regional Evidence

Region-specific analysis depends in part on the quality, relevance and detail of the sources available.

Some locations receive extensive coverage from international organisations, researchers, journalists or other reliable sources. Others may be difficult to assess because reporting is limited, access is restricted or information is inconsistent.

A careful assessment may consider:

- Whether the source identifies the particular region or locality
- The date of publication and period covered by the information
- The source's methodology and apparent reliability
- Whether other independent sources support or qualify the reported information
- Whether the material concerns the relevant group or profile
- Whether the source describes a general pattern or an isolated incident
- Any limitations that affect the conclusions that can reasonably be drawn

The absence of detailed reporting about a locality should not automatically be treated as proof that persecution does not occur there. Equally, limited or unverified reports should not be presented as conclusive evidence of widespread persecution.

Where evidence is incomplete or conflicting, an expert should explain those limitations and avoid expressing greater certainty than the material supports.

## Regional Evidence and Internal Relocation Questions

The location of an alleged threat may also be relevant where internal relocation is raised in an asylum appeal.

Depending on the facts and legal issues, the case may require consideration of whether the alleged source of harm operates in a particular locality, whether relevant conditions differ elsewhere and what evidence exists about other parts of the country.

A persecution expert may be asked to explain documented geographical differences or the reach of a particular actor, where those matters fall within their expertise.

However, regional analysis alone does not determine whether internal relocation is legally available or reasonable in an individual case. That assessment may involve several factual and legal considerations, including the applicant's circumstances and the applicable legal framework.

Expert evidence should therefore address the specific country-related questions within its scope rather than purport to determine the ultimate legal issue.

## How Solicitors Can Frame Instructions for Regional Persecution Analysis

Clear instructions can help an expert understand the precise question requiring specialist consideration.

### Identify the relevant location

Instructions should specify the region, city, town or locality relevant to the issue wherever that information is available.

Where the relevant location has changed over time, it may be necessary to distinguish between the place where the alleged events occurred, the applicant's place of residence and any other location relevant to the appeal.

### Explain the issue in dispute

The instructions should identify what needs to be examined. This might concern the treatment of a particular group, the activities of a local actor, differences between regions or the relevance of local conditions to a particular profile.

A focused question can help keep the report within its intended scope.

### Provide relevant background material

The expert should receive the materials necessary to understand the question being asked, including relevant country information and the factual account or assumptions on which the opinion is sought.

Where facts remain disputed, the instructions should make that clear. The expert should not be expected to resolve credibility issues that fall outside their role.

### Ask the expert to identify limitations

Where local reporting is sparse, inconsistent or out of date, the expert may be asked to explain the limitations of the available sources.

This can help the legal representative and decision-maker understand what the evidence supports, what remains uncertain and whether any conclusions require qualification.

### Keep the opinion within the expert's expertise

The subject of regional analysis should correspond to the expert's relevant qualifications and experience. If a question falls outside that expertise, the limits should be acknowledged rather than addressed through unsupported assumptions.

## Questions a Persecution Expert May Be Asked to Consider

The appropriate questions will depend on the circumstances of the case. Examples may include:

- What reliable country information is available about the relevant region or locality?
- What does the available material report about the treatment of the particular group or profile in that area?
- Are there documented differences between conditions in the relevant locality and other parts of the country?
- What information is available about the activities of the alleged state or non-state actor in that area?
- Does the evidence indicate a documented pattern of treatment, or is it limited to particular incidents?
- What are the principal limitations or gaps in the available regional evidence?
- How does the relevant local context relate to the specific country-related question identified in the instructions?

These questions should be adapted to the particular case. They are not a checklist that must be applied to every asylum appeal, and the expert's ability to address them will depend on the evidence and their area of expertise.

## Conclusion

Regional and locality-specific country evidence might be important where an asylum claim concerns circumstances that cannot be fully understood from national-level information alone.

A persecution expert might be able to provide specialist analysis of documented local conditions, regional differences and the context surrounding a particular profile or alleged source of harm. Whether such evidence is useful will depend on the unresolved question, the expert's expertise and the quality and relevance of the available material.

Careful analysis should distinguish between national trends, local evidence and information relating to an individual. It should also acknowledge gaps and uncertainties rather than assume that conditions are uniform across a country.

For legal representatives, the central consideration is whether a specific regional question requires specialist knowledge and whether an expert with the appropriate expertise can address that question within the limits of the available evidence.
